1.0What this app is for

You tell it what the job involves. It writes the production plan.

When a Deal is won, the shop needs to know what to build, who is building it, and roughly when. That used to be a conversation. This app turns it into a form: you answer a handful of questions about the job, and it produces a complete list of production tasks — with an owner and a working-day duration on every one — which it then creates in Zoho Projects.

Think of it as a planning tool rather than a tracking one. Once you generate the project, the shop works out of Zoho Projects. Come back here only if a job needs re-planning from scratch.

1.1Before you start

  • The Deal must be marked Closed Won. There is a button in the app to do this if you forgot.
  • You need to know what the job actually involves — stair types, railing type, infill material. If you are guessing, stop and check the quote.
  • Generating creates a real project. It is not a preview.

2.0Opening it

  1. Open the won Deal in Zoho CRM.

    Any Deal that is Closed Won. If it is not, the app will offer to mark it for you.

  2. Launch the handoff widget.

    It opens over the Deal as a full window. The Deal's details — customer, address, owner — come across automatically.

  3. Check the version, bottom left.

    If it does not match the current release, close and reopen the widget. It updates itself on open.

3.0How the screen works

Questions down the left. The plan they build, on the right.

The two halves are live. Every answer on the left immediately changes the plan on the right — nothing is queued up and applied later, so you can always see what a choice costs before you commit to it.

3.1The left panel asks one thing at a time

Questions appear as you answer them. A question you have answered collapses to a single line showing your answer, with Change beside it — so the panel always shows what you have decided plus the one thing it is asking next, rather than everything at once.

Options that cannot apply to your job disappear rather than sit there greyed out. Pick Full Metal Railing and the other railing types fold away, because you cannot mix them.

3.2Square and round mean different things

A square tick box is a top-level choice. A round one is a sub-option belonging to the choice directly above it — Turning under Wood Spindles, Custom Metal under Metal Spindles, the Wood Rail Cap under Full Metal.

3.3The right panel is the plan

Each block is a section of work that will become a task list in Zoho Projects. Each row inside it is a task. Reading a row left to right: a coloured dot for the production phase, the task name, its working-day duration, and the person it is assigned to.

PhaseWhat it covers
SetupMeasures, drawings, take-offs, scheduling
FabricationOrdering materials, manufacturing, coating
QAChecks and approvals
InstallAnything happening at the customer's site
Wrap-upFollow-up, invoicing, close out

4.0The questions, in order

Six questions before you get to the detail. Each one unlocks the next.

4.1Who owns this file?

The person who will own the project in Zoho Projects. This arrives already answered with the Deal owner, so on a normal job you just read it and move on. Use Change if someone else is running the job.

4.2Is this a new build or a renovation?

A renovation always adds the removal of the existing work. It also asks one extra question, because protecting the customer's flooring and finishes is a paid extra — it is only added to the plan if it was sold. Neither question appears on a Supply Only job, since we are not attending site.

4.3Supply and install, or supply only?

This is the single most consequential answer on the screen. Supply Only means we never attend site: no measures, no installs, no delivery. The customer collects. Choosing it removes every site task from the plan and replaces the delivery end of the job with Package for Customer Pickup and Customer Pickup Complete.

Worth knowing

On a Supply Only job the app also stops asking questions that could not have an answer — the site measure and install options vanish from the stair section, and the Google review request drops off the follow-up, because we do not chase a review from someone who collected parts.

4.4Does this job include stairs?

Answer No and the stair sections stay out of your way. Nothing you have already ticked is thrown away either — change your mind and it all comes back exactly as it was.

4.5Is there travel on this job?

Answer Yes and the travel window opens. It pulls the job address from the Deal, works out the one-way distance, and past 50 km flags an ESA — which then tags every site task in the plan so the shop can see the site is out of range at a glance.

Once filled in, the question reports the actual answer rather than just "Yes" — for example 120 km each way · ESA · hotel. Change reopens the window.

4.6Does this job include railing?

Answer Yes and the railing section opens beneath. This is where most of the detail lives — see 6.0.

5.0Stairs

One question at a time, from what kind of stair down to whether we install it.

Answer Yes to stairs and it asks what kind. Everything after that follows from your answer, and each step waits for the one before it.

5.1What kind of stairs?

Construction Grade, Finished, or both. A house can have construction-grade stairs downstairs and a finished stair in the main hall, so this is not an either/or — tick both and you get both sets of work.

The question stays open until you pick a stair type, so you always have the chance to add the second grade.

5.2Type, then shape

Pick the type first, and the shape question appears beneath it. Tick as many as apply in each — a job with a straight run and a curved one is one job, and both get tagged onto the manufacturing task so the shop can see it from the task name.

Curved adds time to the build automatically.

5.3Site measure and install

These two appear last, and only on construction grade.

  • Needs Site Measure? adds Complete Site Measure.
  • Install Required? swaps the drop-off for a full install chain. This is an extra charge — construction grade stairs are normally drop-off only.

A finished stair always includes its install, so it is never asked. On a Supply Only job neither question appears at all, because we are not attending site.

5.4Sections fold themselves away

Once a section has its type and shape and you have moved on, it folds to a one-line summary of what you picked — Western, Straight — so the list stays short. Click the header to open it again, and it stays open.

Both scheduling sections arrive unassigned

Const. Stair Scheduling and Finished Stair Scheduling come through with nobody on them, because who runs scheduling is still being decided. Assign them by hand. Every other section has a default.

6.0Railing

First the railing itself, then whatever fills it.

6.1Step 1 — the railing itself

Five choices. Straight and Curved are both traditional framed railings and can share a job. Full Metal Railing and Glass Railing → Frameless cannot be mixed with anything — pick either and the rest fold away, because a job cannot have both. Wall Mount Handrail is secondary and can be added to anything.

6.2Glass, both kinds

Both glass jobs start at the same place. Tick Glass Railing and choose:

  • Framed — glass panels set into a conventional railing. You still need to pick Straight or Curved, because the frame has to be built. The app will tell you if you have not.
  • Frameless — the glass is the barrier, carried on standoffs or a base shoe. There is no railing structure at all, which is why it cannot be combined with anything else.

6.3Step 2 — the infill

What fills the railing: Wood Spindles, Metal Spindles, or Framed Glass Panels. One per job — picking one folds the others away.

Step 2 only appears once you have chosen a railing in Step 1, and it will tell you when there is nothing to ask: a full metal railing is welded complete, and a wall handrail has no spindles.

6.4Sub-options

Three questions appear nested under the choice they belong to, marked with a round tick box.

The welding shop owns a full metal railing outright, infill included. The only thing the wood shop does is the cap rail, and only if you tick it.

7.0The production plan

Everything on the right can be changed before you generate.

The plan is a starting point, not a verdict. If a job needs three days rather than one, change it here — the dates in Zoho Projects are calculated from these numbers.

To do thisDo this
Rename a taskClick the name and type. Your wording is then protected — changing options elsewhere will not overwrite it.
Change a durationClick the number at the end of the row. Half days are allowed — type 0.5.
Assign someoneClick the circle at the end of the row, or the one on the section header to set the whole section.
Clear an assignmentOpen the same picker and choose Unassign.
Delete a taskHover the row and click the × on the right.
Reorder or move a taskDrag it by the handle at the left of the row — including into a different section.
Add a taskAdd task at the bottom of any section.
Remove a whole sectionHover the section header and click the × beside its name.
Durations drive the schedule

Each task starts when the one before it in its lane finishes, weekends skipped. Stairs and railing run as two parallel lanes. A half-day task still occupies a whole day on the calendar, because Zoho Projects has no concept of half a working day — the duration recorded stays exact.

8.0Generating

  1. Read the plan once, top to bottom.

    Check the sections match the job and nothing obvious is missing. This is the last cheap moment to fix it.

  2. Check every task has a person.

    The two stair scheduling sections arrive blank on purpose. Anything else unassigned is worth a second look.

  3. Click Generate Production Run.

    It creates the project in Zoho Projects with all task lists, tasks, owners and dates, and writes a note on the Deal with a direct link to it.

  4. Follow the link and confirm it landed.

    The link appears in the Deal's Notes, posted under the Deal owner's name.

Generating twice

Running it again on the same Deal will not create a duplicate project — it re-uses the one already there. It is safe to retry if something failed part-way.

9.0Drafts, sharing and Reset

Your work saves as you go. Close the widget mid-job and it will be where you left it when you come back.

Save Draft shares that draft with everyone else on the Deal, so a colleague opening the same Deal picks up your version rather than starting again.

Reset clears everything and starts over — your draft and the shared one. It cannot be undone, and it only takes one press.

10.0For the shop floor

Once the handoff runs, the job lives in Zoho Projects. This is how to read what turns up.

Nothing here needs the handoff app. If a task looks wrong, go back to whoever ran the handoff — do not try to fix it by running the handoff again.

10.1Where the work lands

Every job creates one project with up to two task lists:

  • Stair Production & Install — everything to do with stairs
  • Railing Production & Install — railing, glass, welding, coating

They run as two parallel lanes. Railing work does not wait for stair work to finish, and vice versa. Within a lane, each task starts when the one before it ends, weekends skipped.

10.2Reading a task name

Task names carry tags that tell you something the task name alone would not.

TagWhereMeans
[Supply & Install]Front of every taskWe are attending site on this job
[Supply Only]Front of every taskNo site work at all — the customer collects. If you see this on a task that sounds like site work, flag it
[ESA]After the scope tagExtended service area. The site is past 50 km — travel is chargeable and needs planning
[CURVED]After the scope tagCurved work. Expect it to take longer than the straight equivalent
[Straight, Eastern]End of the nameThe shapes and types on that particular job, so you know what you are building without opening the Deal
Example

[Supply & Install] [ESA] Manufacture Stair Package [Straight, Western]

A straight western stair, we are installing it, and the site is out of range.

10.3Reading the phases

Sections arrive in production order, and every task belongs to a phase — the same five listed in 3.3. Roughly: setup and drawings first, then fabrication, with a QA gate before anything leaves the shop, then install, then wrap-up.

The QA tasks are deliberate gates, not paperwork. QA Materials sits before manufacturing starts; QA - Manufactured Materials sits before anything ships.

10.4Dates and durations

Every task has a working-day duration set by whoever ran the handoff, and dates calculated from it. Those are a plan, not a promise — they assume work starts the day the handoff was generated and nothing waits.

Two durations are lead times rather than work: Receive Glass at 14 days and Receive Powder Coating at 10 days are time spent waiting on someone else. If either slips, everything after it in that lane slips too.

10.5If something is wrong

  • A task is missing — tell whoever ran the handoff. They tick the option and it appears next time. Do not add it by hand without telling them, or the next job will be missing it too.
  • A task is assigned to the wrong person — reassign it in Projects. If it is wrong every time, that is a default worth fixing.
  • A duration is consistently wrong — say so. The defaults are editable and several already came from the shop.
  • Site work on a Supply Only job, or the reverse — that is a bug, not a mistake. Screenshot it.

What you seeWhat it means
A change you were told about is not thereCheck the version bottom left. If it is behind, close and reopen the widget — it updates on open.
Nothing appears on the rightA railing type on its own does not build anything until the job has enough detail. On Framed glass, you also need Straight or Curved.
An option you want is missingIt has been folded away because it contradicts something already ticked. Untick that and it returns.

Reporting a problem

Include the version from the bottom left, which Deal it was, and what you had ticked. Those three things are almost always enough to reproduce it.
