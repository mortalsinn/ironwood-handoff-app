1.0What this app is for

The app builds a production plan from a description of the job.

When a Deal is won, the shop needs to know what to build, who is building it, and when. This app collects that information as a set of questions. From the answers it produces a list of production tasks, each with an owner and a working-day duration, and creates them in Zoho Projects.

The app is a planning tool. Once the project is generated, the shop works from Zoho Projects. Return to the app only if a job needs to be re-planned from the beginning.

1.1Before you start

  • The Deal must be marked Closed Won. The app provides a button to set this.
  • The job details should be confirmed before starting: stair grade and type, railing shape, and railing system. These are recorded on the quote.
  • Generating creates the project in Zoho Projects immediately.

2.0Opening it

  1. Open the won Deal in Zoho CRM.

    Any Deal marked Closed Won. If the Deal is not yet marked, the app offers to set it.

  2. Launch the handoff widget.

    It opens over the Deal as a full window. The Deal's details — customer, address, owner — come across automatically.

  3. Check the version, bottom left.

    If the version does not match the current release, close and reopen the widget. The widget checks for updates each time it opens.

3.0How the screen works

Questions on the left; the plan they build on the right.

The two halves update together. Each answer on the left changes the plan on the right immediately, so the effect of every choice is visible before the project is generated.

3.1The left panel asks one thing at a time

Questions appear one at a time. An answered question collapses to a single line showing the answer, with Change beside it. The panel therefore shows the answers given so far and the next question, rather than the whole form.

Options that cannot apply to the job are hidden rather than disabled. Selecting Full Metal (Welded) folds the other railing systems away, since a railing uses one system. The shape, straight or curved, is a separate question and is unaffected.

3.2Square and round mean different things

A square tick box is a top-level choice. A round one is a sub-option belonging to the choice directly above it — Turning under Wood Spindles, Custom Metal under Metal Spindles, the Wood Rail Cap under Full Metal.

3.3The right panel is the plan

Each block is a section of work. Its tasks land in one of the project's two task lists — Stair Production & Install or Railing Production & Install — in the order the blocks appear here. Each row inside a block is a task. The job's scope is stated once as a chip at the top of the panel rather than repeated on every row — it is put back on the task names that reach Zoho Projects. Reading a row left to right: a coloured dot for the production phase, the task name, its working-day duration, and the person it is assigned to.

PhaseWhat it covers
SetupMeasures, drawings, take-offs, scheduling
FabricationOrdering materials, manufacturing, coating
QAChecks and approvals
InstallAnything happening at the customer's site
Wrap-upFollow-up, invoicing, close out

4.0The questions, in order

Six questions precede the job detail. Each one opens the next.

4.1Who owns this file?

The person who will own the project in Zoho Projects. It is completed automatically with the Deal owner. Use Change to select a different person.

4.2Is this a new build or a renovation?

A renovation asks two further questions, because both are chargeable extras rather than things every renovation includes. Was site protection sold? covers protecting the customer's flooring and finishes. Was demolition and disposal sold? covers taking the existing railing out and hauling it away. Answering No to the second leaves both tasks off the plan entirely, for a job where the opening has already been cleared. It also asks one extra question, because protecting the customer's flooring and finishes is a paid extra — it is only added to the plan if it was sold. Site protection is not asked on a Supply Only job, as there is no site attendance — and the removal tasks a renovation adds are site work, so they drop off the plan too.

4.3Supply and install, or supply only?

This is the answer that affects more of the plan than any other. Supply Only means no site attendance: no measures, no installs and no delivery. The customer collects the work. Choosing it removes every site task from the plan and replaces the delivery end of the job with Package for Customer Pickup and Customer Pickup Complete.

On Supply Only jobs

On a Supply Only job the app also omits questions that do not apply. The travel question is skipped, the site measure and install options are removed from the stair section, and the Google review request is removed from the follow-up.

4.4Is there travel on this job?

Asked on Supply & Install jobs only — there is no site to travel to on a Supply Only job.

Answer Yes and the travel window opens. It pulls the job address from the Deal, works out the one-way distance, and past 50 km it flags an ESA, which tags every site task in the plan to indicate the site is outside the service area.

Once filled in, the question reports the actual answer rather than just “Yes” — for example 120 km each way · ESA · hotel. Change reopens the Yes / No; clicking Yes reopens the travel window so you can correct the distance or the hotel.

Changing the answer to No

Close the travel window without saving and the question reads Yes - not filled in yet. Use Change to bring the Yes / No back and answer No. Answering No clears the distance, the hotel and the ESA flag, so nothing is left behind on the job.

4.5Does this job include stairs?

Answer No and the stair sections are hidden. Any selections already made are retained; answering Yes again restores them unchanged.

4.6Does this job include railing?

Answer Yes and the railing section opens beneath. It contains the majority of the job detail; see 6.0.

5.0Stairs

The stair questions run in sequence, from the kind of stair to whether it is installed.

Answering Yes to stairs opens the question of what kind. Each subsequent question depends on the previous answer and appears once it is given.

5.1What kind of stairs?

Construction Grade, Finished, or both. A house may have construction-grade stairs in one area and a finished stair in another. Ticking both produces both sets of work.

The question remains open until a stair type is selected, so the second grade can still be added.

5.2Type, then shape

Select the type first; the shape question then appears beneath it. Tick as many as apply in each. A job may include both a straight run and a curved one, and both are tagged onto the manufacturing task name.

Curved adds time to the build automatically.

5.3Site measure and install

These two appear last, and only on construction grade.

  • Needs Site Measure? adds Complete Site Measure.
  • Install Required? replaces the drop-off with a full install chain. The drop-off tasks, including the theft-documentation photos, are removed, and on a renovation the existing stair removal moves into the install block. This is an extra charge; construction grade stairs are normally drop-off only.

A finished stair always includes its install, so the question is not asked. On a Supply Only job neither question appears, as there is no site attendance.

5.4Sections fold once answered

Once a section has its type and shape and the next question is answered, it folds to a one-line summary of the selections, for example Western, Straight. Clicking the header reopens it, and it remains open.

Both scheduling sections arrive unassigned

Const. Stair Scheduling and Finished Stair Scheduling come through with nobody on them, because who runs scheduling is still being decided. Assign them by hand. Every other section has a default.

6.0Railing

Two questions: what shape the railing is, then what it is made of.

The railing section asks for the shape first and the system second. The two are independent, and any combination is permitted. Once both are answered, the production plan is built on the right.

6.1Step 1 — Railing Shape

Step 1. Railing Shape asks how the railing runs. Tick Straight, Curved, or both if the job has one of each.

Where curved work affects a task's duration, the app applies the additional time. See 6.5.

Step 2. Railing System appears once this question is answered.

6.2Step 2 — Railing System

Once a shape is ticked, Step 2. Railing System appears with five choices:

  • Wood Spindles
  • Metal Spindles
  • Glass Railing — then framed or frameless, see 6.3
  • Full Metal (Welded) — welded complete: spindles, panels and top rail
  • Wall Mount Handrail

One system per job. Ticking one folds the others away. To change the selection, tick the correct system; the previous one is cleared automatically.

Wall Mount Handrail is secondary and may be added to any job, so it remains available alongside any system.

6.3Glass: framed or frameless

Tick Glass Railing and two more boxes appear:

  • Framed — panels set into posts and a top rail. The railing is built first, then the glass is templated off it.
  • Frameless — the glass is the barrier, carried on standoffs or a base shoe. There is no railing structure.

The two are built differently, so this question must be answered before generating.

6.4Full Metal, and the wood cap

On a Full Metal (Welded) railing the welding shop builds the whole thing — spindles, panels and top rail. There is no infill to choose.

A wood top rail is the one exception. Ticking Wood Rail Cap Add-On, nested underneath, assigns the cap rail to the wood shop. Left unticked, the job remains entirely with the metal shop.

6.5Curved works with any system

Shape and system are independent, so every combination is available: a curved welded railing, a straight frameless glass run, a curved wood railing.

Where the shape affects the build, the plan says so. Tasks affected by curved work are tagged [CURVED] for the shop and the install crew. One task adds time automatically: the wood shop's Manufacture Railing Package, which gains two days. The welded, custom-metal and install tasks carry the tag but keep their standard duration, and can be adjusted on the right if the job requires it.

6.6Nested questions

Four questions appear underneath the choice they belong to, marked with a round tick box instead of a square one. Framed and Frameless are the fourth — they sit under Glass Railing and are covered in 6.3.

7.0The production plan

Everything on the right can be changed before generating.

The plan can be edited before it is generated. If a task needs three days rather than one, change it here; the dates in Zoho Projects are calculated from these numbers.

To do thisDo this
Rename a taskClick the name and type. The edited wording is retained, and changing options elsewhere will not overwrite it.
Change a durationClick the number at the end of the row. Half days are allowed — type 0.5.

Durations drive the schedule

Each task starts when the one before it in its lane finishes, weekends skipped. Stairs and railing run as two parallel lanes. A half-day task occupies a whole day on the calendar, as Zoho Projects does not support half working days. The recorded duration remains exact.

7.1The job summary

Once the job has enough answers to build a plan, a Job Summary card appears at the bottom of the left panel and keeps up as you change things:

  • Projected finish — the date the last task ends. Stair and railing run as parallel lanes, weekends are skipped, and it assumes the job starts today. It is the same calculation the handoff itself uses, so it matches the dates Zoho Projects is given.
  • Working days — every task duration on the plan added together.
  • The plan — how many blocks and tasks were built.
  • Owners — how many tasks still have nobody on them. It reads Check owners in amber until none are left, then Ready. Click the count to jump straight to the first task with nobody on it.
  • A bar showing where the time goes across setup, fabrication, QA, install and wrap-up. Hover a slice and everything on the right that is not in that phase dims, so you can see which tasks it means.

The card is a summary of the plan on the right; it performs no separate calculation.

Folding the sections away

Click into Production Notes and the description sections fold away to their one-line summaries, leaving the summary and the notes on screen. Folding is not clearing — click any section header to reopen it.

The dates are a starting point

The dates assume the job starts today and that nothing waits on work outside this plan. They can be adjusted in Zoho Projects afterwards. The summary is provided to review the overall shape of the job before it is created, and is not a commitment to a completion date.

8.0Generating

  1. Read the plan once, top to bottom.

    Confirm the sections match the job and that nothing is missing. Changes made here are simpler than changes made after the project is created.

  2. Check every task has a person.

    The two stair scheduling sections are unassigned by design. Any other unassigned task should be reviewed before generating.

  3. Click Generate Production Run.

    It creates the project in Zoho Projects with all task lists, tasks, owners and dates, and writes a note on the Deal with a direct link to it.

  4. Follow the link and confirm it landed.

    The link appears in the Deal's Notes, posted under the Deal owner's name.

Generating twice

Running it again on the same Deal does not create a duplicate project; the existing project is re-used. Retrying is safe if a previous run did not complete.

9.0Drafts, sharing and Reset

Work is saved automatically. Closing the widget part-way through and reopening it restores the current state.

Save Draft shares the draft with everyone else on the Deal, so a colleague opening the same Deal continues from the same version.

Reset clears the form, the local draft and the shared draft. It takes effect on a single press and cannot be undone.

10.0For the shop floor

Once the handoff runs, the job is held in Zoho Projects. This section explains how to read it.

None of this requires the handoff app. If a task appears incorrect, raise it with the person who ran the handoff. Running the handoff again will not correct it.

10.1Where the work lands

Every job creates one project with up to two task lists:

  • Stair Production & Install — everything to do with stairs
  • Railing Production & Install — railing, glass, welding, coating

The two lists run as parallel lanes. Railing work does not wait for stair work, and stair work does not wait for railing. Within a lane, each task starts when the previous one ends, with weekends skipped.

10.2Reading a task name

Task names carry tags giving information that the name alone does not.

TagWhereMeans
[Supply & Install]Front of every taskWe are attending site on this job
[Supply Only]Front of every taskNo site work; the customer collects. This tag on a site-work task should be reported
[ESA]After the scope tagExtended service area. The site is past 50 km — travel is chargeable and needs planning
[CURVED]After the scope tagCurved work, which generally takes longer than the straight equivalent
[Western, Straight]End of the nameThe types and shapes on that job, type first, so the work can be identified without opening the Deal
Example

[Supply & Install] [ESA] Manufacture Stair Package [Straight, Western]

A straight western stair, installed by us, on a site outside the service area.

10.3Reading the phases

Sections arrive in production order, and every task belongs to a phase — the same five listed in 3.3. Roughly: setup and drawings first, then fabrication, with a QA gate before anything leaves the shop, then install, then wrap-up.

The QA tasks are checkpoints in the sequence. QA Materials occurs before manufacturing begins, and QA - Manufactured Materials before anything ships.

10.4Dates and durations

Every task has a working-day duration set by whoever ran the handoff, and dates calculated from it. The dates are planned rather than confirmed; they assume work begins on the day the handoff was generated and that nothing waits.

Two durations are lead times rather than work: Receive Glass at 14 days and Receive Powder Coating at 10 days represent time waiting on a supplier. A delay to either moves everything after it in that lane.

10.5If something is wrong

  • A task is missing — report it to the person who ran the handoff, so the option can be ticked on future jobs. Adding the task by hand corrects the current job only.
  • A task is assigned to the wrong person — reassign it in Projects. If the same task is wrong on every job, the default should be changed.
  • A duration is consistently wrong — report it. The defaults are editable, and several were set by the shop.
  • Site work on a Supply Only job, or the reverse — this indicates a fault in the app. Please include a screenshot when reporting it.
