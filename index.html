Release Notes

v14.64 Live

v14.64 - Railing Is Frame, Shape, Then Infill

  • Step 1 Is the System: Wood (top rail, shoe and posts), Metal (welded frame — posts, top rail, bottom rail) or Glass (free standing). Wall Mount Handrail sits with them as a secondary item.
  • Step 2 Is the Shape, and Step 3 is the infill: wood or metal spindles, glass panels, or cable.
  • Every System Is Asked the Same Way: a metal frame takes an infill exactly as a wood one does. The welding shop still builds and installs a metal frame outright, so there is no second install visit.
  • Glass Reads Off the Infill: Glass with panels is a framed job; Glass with no infill is free standing on standoffs or a base shoe.
  • Cable Is New: it follows the metal-spindle build and install sequence until the shop gives it its own.

v14.63 - Scope Is Asked Before the Renovation Extras

  • New Order: new build or renovation, then supply and install or supply only, then site protection and demolition, then travel, stairs and railing.
  • Why It Reads Better: scope decides whether we attend site at all, which is what makes the two renovation extras worth asking. Asking them first meant answering two questions that Supply Only then made irrelevant.

v14.62 - Clean Up After the Rebuild

  • Two Unassign Buttons Became One: the picker had a second one that cleared only the row it was on, with no cascade to the tasks under a section. That is why clearing sometimes appeared to do nothing.
  • Custom Railing Shapes Open Step 2: adding your own shape unlocked the plan but never revealed the system question, so the job could not be finished.
  • One Rule, One Place: who owns a section was worked out in four places and tested in five. It is now two small helpers, which is what stops the answers drifting apart.

v14.61 - The Card Counts What You Can See

  • Sections With Nobody On Them Are Counted: a section header reading Assign User now shows on the card. It was counting tasks only, and every task carries its own copy of the default owner, so a section could sit empty while the card read All assigned.
  • It Says Which: for example 1 section, 6 tasks need an owner. Clicking it jumps to the first section with nobody on it.

v14.60 - Taking Someone Off a Section Takes Them Off Its Work

  • Unassign Means Unassign: on a section as well as a task. It used to revert to the department lead, which put the same person straight back.
  • Sections Carry Their Tasks: every task holds its own copy of the default owner, so emptying a section header alone changed nothing. Clearing a section now clears the tasks under it, and assigning one sets them all.
  • The Card Keeps Up: remove Joel from QA - Install Package and it reads 1 still to assign with the badge on Check owners, through every later redraw of the plan.

v14.59 - A Task Can Now Be Left With Nobody

  • The Button Now Says Which Job It Is Doing: on a task it reads Unassign — leave this task with nobody on it. On a section it still reads Clear Override — revert to default department lead, which is the right behaviour there.
  • Why Clearing Looked Broken: there was only ever one button, and it reverted to the department lead. Clearing a task put the default straight back, so the task stayed assigned and the summary correctly said so. There was no way to leave a task unowned at all.

v14.58 - Removing Someone From a Task Now Means Something

  • The Clear Sticks: role defaults refill anything reading UNASSIGNED, and they run on every redraw of the plan, so removing a person put them straight back. A deliberate unassign is now remembered and left alone.
  • It Reaches Zoho: a cleared task used to fall back to its section's owner, so it still arrived assigned to them. It now goes across with nobody on it.
  • The Summary Reflects It: the count goes up and the badge returns to Check owners. Assigning someone again clears the flag.

v14.57 - Demolition and Disposal Is a Question

  • Asked, Not Assumed: Was demolition and disposal sold? now sits under site protection on a renovation. Both are chargeable extras, so both are asked the same way.
  • Answer No and Neither Task Appears: no removal and no disposal, for a job where the old railing is already out.
  • Answer Yes and the Order Is Right: site protection, then removal, then disposal.

v14.56 - Protect the Floors First

  • Site Protection Now Leads the Group: the order was removal, disposal, then protection — the floors were being covered after the old railing came out. Protection now comes first in all three railing chains.

v14.55 - Five Notes From the Floor

  • Disposal Is Its Own Task: a renovation now adds Existing Railing Disposal alongside the removal. Taking the old railing out and hauling it away are separate trips.
  • Shorter Review Task: "Confirm with Accounting - Google Review Request" is now Confirm Google Review Request.
  • No Drop Off When We Are Installing: ticking Install Required on construction grade stairs removed the install-only tasks but left the drop-off and the theft photos in place. The whole drop-off block now goes, and on a renovation the existing stair removal moves into the install block so it is not lost.
  • The Summary Counts Owners Properly: it only looked at each task's own assignee, so tasks covered by their block's owner still counted as unassigned. It now resolves the owner the same way the handoff does.

v14.54 - Full Metal Is Called Full Metal

  • Named After the Product, Not the Process: the chain read Welding Setup & Design, Welding Site Measure, Schedule Welded Install — a word that appears nowhere on the form. Every other chain is named after what is being built, so this one now is too.
  • Ten Names Changed: five block titles and five task names. Nothing moved, nothing was added or removed, and no owner or day count changed.
  • Still the Welding Shop's Work: Custom Metal Fabrication (Welding Shop) keeps the shop's name, because there it identifies who does it.

v14.53 - The Job Summary Actually Shows Up

  • It Appears As Soon As There Is a Plan: it used to require every description section to be folded, which only happened if you clicked into Production Notes. The notes are optional, so on most jobs the card never appeared at all.
  • It Keeps Up: it now re-reads the plan whenever the plan changes, so editing a duration or deleting a task updates the finish date and totals.

v14.52 - The Summary Card Does Something

  • Hover a Slice of the Bar: everything on the right that is not in that phase dims, so "where the time goes" points at real tasks instead of being a number you take on trust.
  • Click "Still to Assign": it jumps to the first task with nobody on it and flashes it, rather than telling you a count and leaving you to hunt.
  • The Finish Date Says How Far Out It Is: "about 6 weeks out" beside the date, because a date on its own does not tell you whether that is soon.

v14.51 - Sweep Before the Manager Review

  • Missing Tailwind Utilities Declared: the inlined build is a subset, and a class it does not contain does nothing at all. Eleven that the app actually uses are now written by hand, including the ones that left the summary card with a light-grey border and no fill.
  • Summary Card Reads Properly in Light Mode: its labels were at 1.98:1. The card now sets its own colours in both themes.
  • Delete Section Button Was Unstyled: it carried two class attributes, so the browser used the first and threw the rest away.
  • Help Corrected: it still listed stairs before travel, and described the pre-v14.47 railing rules.

v14.50 - The Job Summary

  • Click Into Production Notes and the Job Folds Away: the description sections collapse to their one-line summaries. Nothing is cleared — click a header to reopen it.
  • A Summary Card Takes the Space: projected finish date, total working days, how many blocks and tasks, how many still need an owner, and a bar showing where the time goes across setup, fabrication, QA, install and wrap-up.
  • The Dates Are the Real Ones: it replays the same working-day lane maths the handoff itself uses — parallel stair and railing lanes, weekends skipped — so the finish date matches what Zoho Projects will be given.
  • Full Instruction Manual Moved to the Top of Help: and it is visible in light mode again. The button had no background at all, so white text sat on a white panel and only appeared on hover.

v14.49 - You Can Answer No to Travel Again

  • Change Reopens the Question: answering Yes to travel and then closing the travel window without filling it in left the answer stuck on Yes. Change jumped straight back into the travel window, so there was no way to say No. Change now reopens the Yes / No.
  • Yes Still Reaches the Details: clicking Yes reopens the travel window even when Yes is already selected.
  • No Clears It Properly: answering No wipes the distance, hotel and ESA flag, so a mistake cannot leave travel on the job.

v14.48 - Step 3 Removed, and Three Fixes Behind It

  • Step 3. Extras Is Gone: Turning Required and Wood Rail Cap Add-On now sit under the system they belong to, which left Step 3 as a heading over an empty list. Removed.
  • Section Numbers Corrected: a railing-only job showed 4. Railing Assembly and 5. Production Notes instead of 1 and 2. Removing Step 3 left two references behind and the resulting error stopped the renumbering.
  • Folded Summary Reads Cleanly: collapsing Step 1 on a curved job printed the option and its description run together. It now shows the option only.
  • Note Under Curved Removed: curved carries its own day counts and the shop knows it.

v14.47 - Shape and System Are Two Questions

  • Step 1 Is Now Railing Shape: Straight or Curved, tick both if the job has one of each. It describes how the railing runs, nothing else.
  • Step 2 Is Railing System: Wood Spindles, Metal Spindles, Glass, Full Metal and Wall Mount now sit together in one list, because they are all answers to the same question - what is it made of.
  • Curved Works With Everything: Straight and Curved used to be railing types, so ticking Curved ruled out Full Metal and glass. A curved welded railing is an ordinary job and can now be described as one - and curved adds build time to whichever system you pick, not just to wood.
  • Nothing Changed on the Right: every existing combination generates exactly the same blueprint it did in v14.46, verified block by block against the previous build.

v14.46 - Travel Moves Up, and the Manual Ships With the App

  • Travel Is Asked Early: it is a fact about the job, not about its components, so it now sits with the other job questions rather than halfway down between stairs and railing.
  • Skipped on Supply Only: along with site protection. We are not attending site, so neither question has an answer that means anything.
  • The Manual Needs No Sign-In: it now ships with the app itself, so Help → Full instruction manual just opens for anyone. The Download PDF button is still on it.

v14.45 - Site Protection Is Sold, Not Assumed

  • Now a Question: protecting the customer's flooring and finishes is a paid extra, and every renovation was getting it automatically. A renovation now asks whether it was sold, and only adds the task if it was.
  • Fix — Metal and Glass Renovations Had No Removal: the removal of the existing railing lived inside the wood-shop section, so a full metal or frameless renovation never got one. Both now do, without doubling up on a job that has a wood railing as well.
  • Help Rewritten: it described an older version of the app. It now covers the question order, the combinations that fold away, what the round tick boxes mean, and why stair scheduling arrives unassigned — with a link to the full illustrated manual.

v14.44 - Railing Type Folds Once You Pick the Infill

  • Except on Full Metal and Frameless: neither has an infill to pick, and Full Metal's wood cap question lives inside Step 1 — folding it would hide a question that still needs answering.
  • Fix — ESA Could Tag a Job With No Travel: the ESA flag was read straight off the travel form rather than from the travel question, so a restored draft could carry it onto every site task of a job you had answered No to.

v14.43 - You Can Tick Both Stair Grades Again

    How to use the Handoff App

    Full instruction manual

    Every screen in depth, with screenshots and a reference of what each option builds. Opens in a new tab, no sign-in needed, and has a Download PDF button at the top.

    1. Answer the questions, it writes the plan

    The left side asks one question at a time. Each answer folds down to a single line and opens the next question, so you are never looking at the whole form at once. The right side builds the production plan as you go — those sections become tasks in the project's two task lists, Stair and Railing.

    Nothing is chosen for you. The only pre-filled answer is the file owner, and that comes from the Deal.

    2. The order it asks in

    • Who owns this file — becomes the project owner in Zoho Projects.
    • New build or renovation — a renovation adds removal, and asks whether site protection was sold.
    • Supply & Install or Supply Only — the biggest answer on the form. Supply Only strips every site task and ends with Customer Pickup.
    • Travel — past 50km it flags an ESA and tags every site task. Skipped on Supply Only.
    • Stairs — then what kind, then type, then shape, then site measure and install.
    • Railing — the system first, then the shape, then the infill.

    3. Impossible combinations disappear

    • One railing system per job — pick Wood, Metal, Glass or Full Metal and the others fold away. The shape is a separate question: Straight and Curved can share a job, and either works with any system.
    • Wall Mount Handrail is secondary and can be added to any job.
    • One infill per railing. Picking one folds the others away.
    • A round tick box means the option belongs to the one above it — turning under wood spindles, custom metal under metal spindles, the wood cap under full metal.
    • Unticking something never destroys what was underneath it. Change your mind and it all comes back.

    4. Who owns what

    • Tasks pre-assign to the team's default leads from the live roster.
    • Sales sections default to the Deal owner.
    • Both stair scheduling sections arrive unassigned on purpose — who runs scheduling is still being decided. Pick someone before you generate.
    • Click the circle on a section header to set the whole section, or the one on a task to set just that task.

    5. Timelines

    • Every task carries its working days — click the number to change it. Half days are allowed.
    • Stairs and railing run as two parallel lanes; within a lane each task follows the one before it, weekends skipped.
    • Two numbers are waiting, not work: Receive Glass at 14 days and Receive Powder Coating at 10.

    6. Drafts and Reset

    Your work saves as you go. Save Draft shares it with everyone else on the Deal. Reset clears everything and starts over — one press, and it cannot be undone.
